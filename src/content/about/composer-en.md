Almost everything I write starts with a need: a choir, an ensemble, an event, players at a particular level, and a
date; I'm creating something that has to work. But there's always something of mine in it too, the way a joke between
friends isn't the best joke in the world, but it's ours.

For years I wondered whether you can write music with algorithms without losing control of the music. I finally gave
all those years of trials and questions a shape in my degree thesis,
[*Musical creation as an algorithm*](/en/articles/musical-creation-as-an-algorithm/). As with most things in music,
the answer depends, and the interesting part is what it depends on.

I owe all this, among many other friends, to three teachers: **Vicente Cuadrado**, for welcoming me into music;
**Jordi Blanch**, for teaching me that music is lived; and **Marc García**, for helping me write when I didn't believe
I could.
